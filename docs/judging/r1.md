# Judging release r1: pre-registered rule

Registered 2026-10-25, 25 hours into the record (it began with issue 498020, 2026-10-24 20:00 UTC), before any
result could support a claim. From the first issue sealed after this file was merged, every manifest carries this
file's keccak256 hash, so the chain shows the rule was fixed before the results it judges. The rule is never changed:
a revision would be a new file with its own start issue, and this one would still be reported as written.

## The question
Does the Demon of release r1 forecast the 1- and 4-hour questions better than the best classical volatility models?

- **Judged:** the Demon, `0x97bc9657cb5b4af73b46e0031ac385cab039e72fb9fb8edf591bd457f149914e` (a linear pool with
  fixed weights; see [`engine/releases/r1`](../../engine/releases/r1/release.json)).
- **Opponents:** GARCH(1,1)-t, `0xdf33f0170700f1581334e893a7d08329d5a407c5b0e2913a2c4b9903c7fbe814`, and HAR-RV,
  `0x564e31cf84b39fb54ab6fe6e61213eaf70f7cccc792bde19321d357941662dc5`, both of release r1. Each is tested
  separately.
- **Why these questions:** in the PoC 2 backtest, the pool's edge over both baselines was clear of zero at 1 and 4
  hours and unresolved at 24 hours ([PoC 2](../research/2026-10-24-poc2-evolution.md)). This rule tests that
  hypothesis on fresh, pre-committed data.

## The statistic
- **Score:** the binned log score of each committed forecast (basis points / 10,000) on its realized bin, settled as
  in [the live record](../design/live-record.md#settlement-and-scores).
- **Per issue:** the mean, over the seven assets and the 1- and 4-hour horizons, of the Demon's score minus the
  opponent's. An issue counts once both horizons have settled and both forecasters forecast all 14 of its questions.
  Issues where either one is missing (a gap, a failed forecaster) are left out and counted. Nothing else is left out,
  re-weighted or restarted.
- **Gain:** the mean of the per-issue values, each issue weighted equally.
- **Uncertainty:** a circular block bootstrap over issues in order (2,000 resamples, seed 0), with blocks of 72
  issues and, as a check, 168 issues. z is the gain divided by the standard deviation of the resampled gains.

## The decision
- Three scheduled looks. Look k uses the issues of the first d days of the record (issues 498020 to
  498020 + 24·d − 1), once their 4-hour questions have settled:

  | look | d | uses issues up to | computed after (UTC) | the Demon beats the opponent if |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 90 | 500179 | 2027-01-23 00:00 | z ≥ 4.36 |
  | 2 | 180 | 502339 | 2027-04-23 00:00 | z ≥ 2.99 |
  | 3 | 365 | 506779 | 2027-10-25 00:00 | z ≥ 1.97 |

- A claim needs z above the threshold with both block lengths. The thresholds spend a one-sided 2.5% error rate per
  opponent across the three looks (Lan-DeMets, O'Brien-Fleming-type spending at information fractions 90/365,
  180/365 and 1).
- "Beats the classical baselines" needs a claim against both GARCH and HAR. Each is a one-sided 2.5% test, so no
  further correction is needed.
- Once a claim is made, it stands. Later looks are still reported. If no look reaches its threshold, the result is
  "no evidence that the Demon beats" that opponent on these questions.
- The mirror claim (the opponent beats the Demon) uses the same thresholds on −z.

## What is published, and when
- **Each look:** the gain, both z values, the number of issues used and left out, and the verdict, whatever it says.
- **Day 30** (issues up to 498739, after 2026-11-24 00:00 UTC): a direction report with the same numbers and no
  verdict either way.
- **Between looks:** the running statistic appears on the scoreboard labelled "running, unresolved". It is never
  presented as a verdict.
- **Reported but never claimed:** the 24-hour questions, all horizons together, each horizon alone, and comparisons
  with naive and EWMA.
- **Out of scope:** other forecasters (shadow candidates, later releases) are exploratory until they get a rule of
  their own.
