The live record

Every hour, Laplace's forecasters put probabilities on how far seven crypto assets will move over the next 1, 4 and 24 hours. The forecasts are committed to Robinhood Chain before any of them can settle, and scored in public against classical baselines that answer the same questions.

Language models write and mutate small forecasting programs, a proper scoring rule judges them on past data, and the survivors are pooled into one forecaster, the Demon. Learned and classical models answer next to it. Nothing on this page is a verdict yet: the record began on 24 October 2026, and the rules that will judge it were fixed before its results.

This hour's odds

What the two forecasters under a pre-registered rule committed for issue …: ten probabilities for the log return from the hour's close to the close 1, 4 or 24 hours later. The outer bins are open. These are the committed numbers, unchanged; whether they are any good is what the scores are for, and they are not trading advice.

The ladder

From the simplest forecaster to the most elaborate. Each rung adds one idea to the ones below it and has to earn it. The classical rungs are strong: on the PoC 2 holdout, EV-HAR alone got almost nine-tenths of the Demon's edge over GARCH at 1 and 4 hours.

Reading the scoreboard…

Live gains are paired: each forecaster against naive on the questions both answered, the 1- and 4-hour questions of an issue together. Forecasters that joined the record later cover fewer and later hours, so read a row against naive, not against the other rows; the rules above compare their forecasters on the same hours. Development evidence comes from backtests that were inspected while the forecasters were built: it is why a forecaster is in the record, not a measure of how good it is.

Check it yourself

Nothing here asks to be trusted. Every hour's forecasts are hashed into a Merkle tree, and its root goes to LaplaceLedger within 15 minutes of the hour, before the shortest question can settle. The ledger only accepts a commitment from the committer, only for an issue after the last one and only inside its window, so the block time proves when the forecasts were sealed.

  1. keccak256 of manifest.json equals the manifest hash in the issue's Committed event, the root equals rootOf(issue), and the block falls inside the issue's window.
  2. The leaves rebuilt from forecasts.json give that root, and every pool's forecasts are its members' forecasts mixed with its weights.
  3. Public programs, with their parameter files, rerun on the inputs the manifest identifies, give the published forecasts.
  4. Once a private program is revealed, its source hashes to its identity and reruns to its record.

The reference verifier does the first two from the public files and the chain:

git clone https://github.com/laplaceprotocol/laplace
cd laplace/engine && uv sync
uv run laplace-engine verify --issue …

The formats (leaves, tree, manifest, settlement) are in the live-record design note.

Or check an issue here

This runs in your browser. It downloads the issue's files from data.laplaceprotocol.com, hashes them with the keccak-256 in this page's own source, and asks Robinhood Chain's public RPC endpoints for the commitment.

What the record does not prove. It proves when each forecast was made and that it was not changed afterwards. It does not prove that nothing else was forecast and discarded: the release fixes the forecasters in advance and every issue lists all of them, failures included, which is what makes selective reporting visible. The committer key is the ledger's only trust assumption; if it were ever compromised, a new ledger would be deployed and announced, and the old record would stand as it is.

Corrections

When we find a mistake, the fix and the numbers it replaces stay side by side. Newest first; the same log is in the repository as docs/corrections.md.

  1. 2026-10-27 Research returns as log price ratios

    Research computed 4- and 24-hour returns as sums of hourly returns, while programs use log price ratios; the two differed by about 1e-15 where a price came back to the same value. Rerun on ratios, EV-NET's gain over EV-HAR stays +0.0084 nats per question (1 hour: +0.0169 instead of +0.0167), and no month moves by more than 0.0003. The note

  2. 2026-10-25 The first day's committed candles were provisional

    Coinbase re-aggregates a just-closed hourly candle a little over five minutes after the hour, and the engine sealed at :00:30. Of the first day's 168 committed last candles, 9 differ from the exchange's final candles, all in the close, by up to 0.19% (LINK, issue 498039). Those issues stand as committed, and settlement uses the committed closes, so scores stay consistent with the record. Since issue 498048 the engine seals at :07, after the re-aggregation. PR #27

  3. 2026-10-25 The event study's comparison hours

    The first event study compared the hours around scheduled releases with all hours, which mostly measures the US session's clock. Against the same UTC hours on weekdays without releases, CPI's next three hours are 1.2x, 0.9x and 1.4x the usual variance, not 2.1x to 2.6x, and the jobs report's 1.0x to 1.3x. The quiet hour before both holds (0.45x and 0.34x). PR #23, the note

  4. 2026-10-24 The GARCH baseline was mis-fitted

    GARCH's Student-t likelihood had an extra 1/ν in its normalizing constant, which pushed ν towards infinity and left its volatility dynamics arbitrary. Fixed and rerun on the same programs and windows: GARCH moved from −0.0016 to +0.0297 over naive, and the evolved pool's edge over it from +0.0426 to +0.0113 (−0.0151 to +0.0312). The claim that the pool clearly beats GARCH was withdrawn. PR #9, the note

  5. 2026-10-24 PoC 2, after an independent review

    Future candles were reachable from a program's context (no program used them); the pool was smoothed twice when scored, which flattered it by about 0.0006 nats per question, and its weights were not fully fitted; window boundaries leaked; intervals were too narrow; and the run cost about $2.9–3.5, not the $2.35 recorded. Each was fixed with regression tests and the report rerun; the claim that the pool's edge over HAR was just clear of zero was withdrawn. PR #4, the note
